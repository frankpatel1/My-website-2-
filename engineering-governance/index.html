Engineering Governance Policy

TrustNexus Technologies | Version: 1.0 | Effective Date: October 2026 | Technical Standards & Operational Controls

Engineering Governance Framework: This policy establishes the principles, quality standards, and technical controls governing system design, installation, commissioning, cybersecurity, life safety, maintenance, and technical change management across TrustNexus client sites.

1. Purpose

This Engineering Governance Policy establishes a framework for planning, designing, installing, integrating, testing, commissioning, and maintaining the systems delivered by TrustNexus Technologies.

Its purpose is to promote consistent engineering practices, documented technical decisions, safe implementation, cybersecurity awareness, quality assurance, and accountable service delivery.

This document is a proposed governance framework. It does not represent a claim that TrustNexus currently holds any specific certification or that every procedure described below is already implemented.

2. Scope

This Policy applies to projects and services involving, where included in the agreed scope:

  • CCTV surveillance and AI video analytics.
  • Access control and physical security barriers.
  • Fire detection and life-safety integration.
  • Intrusion detection and environmental monitoring.
  • Structured cabling and fiber-optic infrastructure.
  • Enterprise networking and related IT infrastructure.
  • Public address and voice evacuation systems.
  • System integration, commissioning, and handover.
  • AMC 2.0 maintenance and technical support.

It applies to relevant employees, engineers, contractors, and approved service providers involved in these activities.

3. Governance Principles

Engineering work should follow these core principles:

3.1 Safety first

Life safety, personnel protection, and compliance with applicable requirements take precedence over convenience or non-essential operational functionality.

3.2 Documented engineering

Design decisions, assumptions, interfaces, configuration changes, test results, and approvals should be documented at a level appropriate to project risk and complexity.

3.3 Integration by design

Systems should be planned as coordinated components of the wider facility infrastructure, with clearly defined interfaces, dependencies, and responsibilities.

3.4 Security by design

Cybersecurity, physical access restrictions, privacy, and authorized data handling should be considered during design and throughout the system lifecycle.

3.5 Lifecycle reliability

Engineering decisions should consider maintainability, equipment availability, support arrangements, obsolescence, and the operational needs of the facility.

3.6 Accountability

Technical approvals, project responsibilities, change authorizations, and acceptance decisions should be assigned to identifiable roles.

4. Roles and Responsibilities

The following responsibilities should be assigned for each project according to its size and complexity:

  • Engineering Lead: Reviews technical requirements and proposed architecture; coordinates system interfaces and engineering deliverables; reviews design changes and technical deviations; ensures testing and handover criteria are defined.
  • Project Manager: Coordinates schedule, resources, site access, and customer communication; maintains the project plan, issue register, and approval records; escalates delivery risks and unresolved dependencies.
  • Installation and Commissioning Team: Follows approved drawings, installation procedures, and site safety requirements; records installation deviations and test results; reports defects, integration issues, and incomplete work.
  • Cybersecurity or IT Responsible Person: Reviews network architecture, access permissions, configuration security, and relevant cyber risks; coordinates approved updates, vulnerability handling, and incident escalation.
  • Customer or Facility Representative: Confirms operational requirements and site-specific constraints; provides authorized access and required approvals; participates in testing, acceptance, and handover; retains responsibility for facility operations.

Where one individual performs multiple roles, responsibilities and approval authority remain clearly defined.

5. Requirements and Project Initiation

Before design or installation begins, the project team establishes the applicable requirements, which may include:

  • Site conditions and operational objectives.
  • Security zones and access-control policies.
  • Surveillance coverage and recording requirements.
  • Fire detection and emergency-response requirements.
  • Network capacity and connectivity requirements.
  • Power supply, UPS, and environmental conditions.
  • Existing equipment and required third-party interfaces.
  • Privacy, data retention, and cybersecurity requirements.
  • Applicable codes, permits, approvals, and testing obligations.
  • Maintenance arrangements and acceptance criteria.

Assumptions, exclusions, and unresolved requirements must be documented and approved before they affect implementation.

6. Design Governance

6.1 Design documentation

Project documentation includes system architecture diagrams, bills of materials, network topology and IP plans, camera layouts, door schedules, fire-alarm interface matrices, cable routes, rack layouts, and commissioning plans.

6.2 Design review

Designs are reviewed by an appropriately qualified person considering functionality, compatibility, safety, capacity, maintainability, cybersecurity, privacy, and legal requirements.

6.3 Technical deviations

Any material deviation from an approved design is recorded with its rationale, impact, and required approval before implementation, except where immediate action is necessary to address an urgent safety risk.

7. OEM and Component Management

Equipment selection considers functionality, architecture compatibility, manufacturer documentation, spare parts availability, firmware lifecycle, warranty conditions, environmental ratings, and regulatory certifications.

The presence of a brand in TrustNexus's OEM integration materials does not, by itself, establish distributor authorization, manufacturer certification, or approval for every product model. Any required OEM authorization or certification should be verified for the specific project.

8. Installation Quality and Site Safety

Installation work is conducted according to approved drawings, manufacturer instructions, National Building Code (NBC) guidelines, and site-safety procedures. Controls address safe access, cable routing, device mounting, electrical isolation, rack organization, and protection of installed assets. Personnel must not bypass required safety controls or perform work without necessary competence.

9. Cybersecurity and Network Governance

Network-connected systems are configured with security controls appropriate to risk:

  • Unique accounts, strong authentication, and least-privilege role separation.
  • Hardening of switches, routers, cameras, controllers, and servers.
  • Controlled remote access and approved encrypted support channels.
  • Documented credential handover and mandatory secure default credential changes.
  • Firmware updates, network segmentation (VLANs), and active logging.

Passwords and credentials are never placed in unencrypted documentation. Security measures are balanced with validated life-safety requirements; changes affecting emergency operation require mandatory safety review.

10. Privacy and Surveillance Governance

CCTV, AI analytics, access control, and biometric deployments are configured exclusively for authorized customer purposes. Governance establishes camera coverage boundaries, authorized personnel privileges, retention/deletion rules, and audit records. Facial recognition and biometric features are enabled only where the customer has established the necessary legal basis and safeguards.

11. Fire and Life-Safety Governance

Fire detection, alarms, access-control fail-safe overrides, and voice evacuation require paramount life-safety attention. Engineering includes approved cause-and-effect matrices, device compatibility checks, verification of fail-safe door behavior during power loss, emergency notification testing, and required statutory sign-offs. General integration capabilities must not be treated as proof of statutory compliance without formal authority approval.

12. Integration and Interoperability

Before connecting disparate systems, the project team identifies supported communication protocols (ONVIF, BACnet, OSDP, Modbus), required drivers, data flows, and network latency requirements. If an interface is unsupported or a function cannot be verified, the limitation is documented and formally communicated to the customer.

13. Testing and Commissioning

Commissioning includes cable continuity and OTDR certification, network throughput checks, camera image and recording verification, door override and credential tests, fire-alarm cause-and-effect validation, and backup power verification. Systems are not represented as fully accepted where material safety criteria remain unresolved.

14. Change Management

Changes to system design, hardware, firmware, network architecture, and operating logic follow a documented change record: description, affected systems, risk assessment, technical approvals, implementation/rollback plans, and updated as-built drawings. Changes to life-safety logic must receive technical and safety sign-offs before live activation.

15. Documentation and Handover

At completion, the project team provides specified handover documentation: as-built drawings, asset registers, network/interface documentation, test reports, operating instructions, OEM warranties, training records, and secure credential handover packages.

16. Maintenance and AMC 2.0 Governance

Maintenance activities follow the applicable support agreement: preventive maintenance schedules, telemetry health checks, service ticket prioritization, remote diagnostics, certified engineer dispatch, and firmware optimization. Any stated uptime commitment (e.g. 99.99%) is evaluated against the specific contract and defined measurement scope.

17. Incident, Defect and Nonconformity Management

Technical incidents, safety concerns, integration failures, and defects are recorded and investigated: initial assessment, immediate containment, customer notification, root-cause investigation, corrective action, and verification. Potentially unsafe conditions are escalated immediately.

18. Supplier and Contractor Governance

Suppliers and contractors are evaluated based on technical competence, qualifications, product authenticity, cybersecurity practices, and safety compliance. Third-party personnel receive only the access necessary to perform authorized work.

19. Training and Competency

Personnel assigned to engineering activities maintain skills and training appropriate to their roles, including product knowledge, installation standards, site safety, cybersecurity practices, privacy protocols, and life-safety procedures.

20. Quality Assurance and Continuous Improvement

Engineering quality is evaluated through defect trends, test results, customer feedback, and post-project reviews to enhance design consistency, integration reliability, documentation accuracy, and safety performance.

21. Performance Reporting

Where included in the service agreement, reporting covers project milestones, commissioning status, service ticket volumes, response and restoration times, maintenance logs, and system availability calculations.

22. Records and Document Control

Engineering records are maintained under version control with appropriate access permissions and retention schedules reflecting legal, contractual, and operational requirements.

23. Exceptions and Escalation

Proposed exceptions to this Policy require documentation of justification, risks, compensating controls, and authorization. Exceptions affecting life safety, privacy, or statutory obligations are prohibited where they would breach applicable law.

24. Review and Approval

This Policy is reviewed periodically and upon material technological, regulatory, or operational changes.

25. Governing Requirements

Engineering activities must follow applicable Indian laws, site safety rules, manufacturer technical manuals, approved project specifications, and relevant technical standards. This Policy establishes an internal governance framework and does not independently replace project-specific engineering approval or statutory certification.

  • Approve project-specific Engineering Governance matrices and assign certified engineering leads.
