Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common questions about our 4-layer living building nervous system, unified network backbones, and PSIM orchestration.

Converged architecture integrates compatible security, safety, networking, and facility-management technologies into a coordinated IP/IoT-based infrastructure. Instead of operating as isolated systems, these technologies can exchange information and support unified monitoring and operations.

TrustNexus describes its approach as a four-layer building intelligence system. It brings together field-level devices, network infrastructure, active technology nodes, and an overarching management and integration layer.

The architecture is presented through four interconnected tiers:

  • Field devices: Sensors, cameras, detectors, and other endpoint equipment that collect information or perform functions.
  • Network infrastructure: The connectivity backbone that carries data between devices and systems.
  • Active nodes: Networked and computing equipment that supports system operation, processing, and communication.
  • Overarching brain: Building Management System (BMS) and integration software that provide a coordinated management layer.

The specific equipment and software depend on the facility's requirements.

Siloed systems can create separate monitoring environments, fragmented workflows, and additional integration challenges. A converged architecture is designed to improve interoperability, centralize visibility, and help facility teams coordinate responses across compatible systems.

Depending on product compatibility and project requirements, a converged facility may bring together CCTV, access control, fire and life safety, network infrastructure, PSIM, and BMS or other management software.

The network backbone connects field devices, active equipment, and management platforms. Properly engineered fibre and copper infrastructure, switching, and redundancy help support reliable communications between the facility's connected systems.

It can enable coordinated monitoring and event correlation across compatible systems. For example, an access-control event may be associated with relevant camera footage, allowing operators to investigate activity through a more unified workflow.

PSIM, or Physical Security Information Management, can act as a centralized operational interface for compatible security systems. It helps operators view events, correlate information, and coordinate responses without having to manage every system independently.

TrustNexus emphasizes reducing single points of failure through suitable redundancy and failover mechanisms. Depending on the system design, this can include self-healing optical rings, redundant recording infrastructure, and resilient addressable fire-system loops.
